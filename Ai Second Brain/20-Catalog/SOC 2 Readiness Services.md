# SOC 2 Readiness Services

A dated, comparable shortlist of the services a small team actually encounters on the way to a SOC 2 report: compliance automation platforms, and the subservice vendors whose reports the organisation carves out. It complements, and does not replace, the SOC 2 skills: use a shortlist row to narrow a tooling or vendor decision, then run the matching skill before writing an ADR or engaging a firm.

## How to read the cost and evidence columns

- **Verified-Official:** fetched from the vendor's own page in the 2026-11-02 research run (via the web index, not a live browser window — see the caveats below).
- **Index:** search-result text or a secondary summary, not opened on the primary page.
- **Estimate:** a third-party or vendor-adjacent range, not a published price list. Do not budget from it without a quote.
- **No catalog note approves a provider, a platform, or an auditor.** Engaging an auditor, sharing customer data with a platform, and publishing any compliance claim remain R3. Recheck pricing, plan gates, report access, and retention limits from the vendor's own pages before an ADR is accepted.

## When not to buy a compliance platform

Do not buy if: (a) no customer has asked for a SOC 2 report or a completed questionnaire that a platform would actually answer; (b) one manual quarter of access reviews, change history, and vendor reviews has not yet been run (the platform would automate a cadence that does not exist); (c) the stack's connectors are unverified (Vercel, Neon, and Clerk evidence still manual anyway); (d) the annual fee exceeds the value of the deals it would unblock; or (e) it would be bought expecting it to "get SOC 2" without an auditor engagement. Buy when: a Type II observation period is committed, evidence volume (people, systems, frameworks) exceeds manual cadence, or an auditor or buyer explicitly expects continuous monitoring evidence. Record the deferral as a dated decision note — itself good evidence that tooling decisions were reasoned, not defaulted. This decision rule is an analyst recommendation from the research run, not an auditor rule.

## Shared-responsibility summary

Vercel runs code safely; Neon stores data safely; Clerk proves who the user is; Stripe moves money safely. None of them prove that the organisation's code authorises the right tenant, that its scene-control actions are authorised, that its tokens are scoped and revocable, or that its changes were reviewed. Those are the controls a SOC 2 auditor will test on the organisation — and they are exactly where a livestream control surface's real risk lives: a leaked streamer token, a cross-tenant scene change during a live show, a forged webhook upgrading a subscription.

## Open questions — recheck before any ADR or auditor engagement

1. Live-browser verification failed in the research run (page gone, no tabs); "verified" claims above rest on index fetches of official pages crawled under a minute before reading on 2026-11-02, not on a live-window read. Re-verify Vercel's official compliance page and any plan-gated report access in a live session before citing them.
2. Vercel, Neon, and Clerk log-retention tiers, Neon point-in-time recovery windows, and report-access plan gates were not verified from current official pricing or docs in that run. Do not cite a retention or recovery window from memory or from a third-party questionnaire — confirm in the respective console and docs at implementation time.
3. The "3-month minimum" Type II observation period is repeated industry guidance, not a verified AICPA rule text in that run. Confirm the minimum, and the period target buyers require (3 versus 6 versus 12 months), with the engaged CPA firm.
4. OneTrust and Tugboat current status rests on a single secondary source (2026-10-20) reporting Certification Automation retirement and migration. Verify with OneTrust before recommending or dismissing it.
5. The AICPA Trust Services Criteria full text is a paid publication; the research run relies on AICPA-adjacent summaries for criterion wording. The engaged auditor's reading of TSP Section 100 governs.
