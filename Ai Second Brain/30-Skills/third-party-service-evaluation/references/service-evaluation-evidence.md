# Service Evaluation Evidence

Verified: 2026-10-21

## Authoritative Sources

- [NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 Update 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/161/r1/upd1/final) addresses cybersecurity supply-chain risk across products and services throughout acquisition, use, and lifecycle. Its federal and enterprise scope should be scaled to project impact.
- [CISA Secure by Demand Guide](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-08/SecureByDemandGuide_080624_508c.pdf) provides customer questions about manufacturer product security, secure defaults, vulnerability handling, transparency, and procurement expectations.
- [GOV.UK: Choosing technology](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/technology/choosing-technology-an-introduction) emphasizes adaptability, security risk, total cost, open standards, data control, prototypes, and avoiding unnecessary lock-in.

## Decision Boundaries

- Compliance reports and certifications can narrow questions but do not prove product configuration, tenant isolation, secure integration, or fitness for a use case.
- Provider status history is backward-looking. Design timeouts, fallback, reconciliation, and user-visible failure behavior for dependency loss.
- A generous free tier can conceal egress, log, support, seat, overage, migration, and operational cost. Model the high scenario and exit.
- Approval is scoped to the reviewed service, plan, region, settings, API, and asset versions. Material changes trigger re-review.
