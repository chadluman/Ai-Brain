# Discovery Evidence

Verified: 2026-10-21

## Public-Service Product Guidance

- [GOV.UK: How the discovery phase works](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works) starts with understanding the problem, users, constraints, policy or organizational intent, related services, cost effectiveness, and success before committing to an alpha. It explicitly treats stopping after discovery as a valid outcome.
- [GOV.UK: Learning about users and their needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs) recommends continuous research, direct observation and interviews, existing evidence, and traceability from stable user needs to specific stories.
- [GOV.UK: Getting the scope of your transaction right](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/scoping-your-service) places a transaction in the user's wider journey and warns against letting organizational or technical boundaries define the service.
- [GOV.UK: Measuring the benefits of your service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/measuring-service-benefits) combines operational data with qualitative evidence and encourages baselines and iterative measurement.

## Interpretation Boundaries

- Government service guidance is a strong public, inspectable reference but includes government-specific policy and approval assumptions. Adapt it to the product and organization.
- User requests, stakeholder opinions, support tickets, market claims, analytics, and interviews each reveal different evidence. Triangulate rather than treating one source as truth.
- A prototype can test comprehension or workflow without proving demand, accessibility at production quality, security, scalability, or willingness to pay.
- MVP means the smallest coherent test of value and risk, not an incomplete product with missing safety or accessibility controls.
