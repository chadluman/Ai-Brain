# Web Discoverability and SEO

## Outcome

Produce a prioritized, evidence-led plan for qualified organic visibility that connects audience needs and original content value to sound crawl, index, rendering, and measurement behavior. Treat ranking as an uncertain outcome to improve and observe, never a guaranteed deliverable.

Read [discoverability evidence](references/discoverability-evidence.md) before asserting crawler or ranking behavior, collecting search evidence, or adding vendor-specific markup. Follow the vault's [Research Standard](../../00-System/Research%20Standard.md), especially its access and collection boundary.

## Inputs

Establish the business outcome and conversion, target audiences and languages, markets and search platforms, current traffic and query baseline, site ownership or data access, public and private content boundaries, content expertise and ownership, key journeys and information needs, URL and rendering architecture, duplicate and filtered views, launch or migration state, performance and accessibility requirements, research permissions and budget, analytics constraints, and the observation window.

## Workflow

1. Define success in business and user terms. Establish a dated baseline for qualified impressions, clicks, landing-page engagement, conversions, crawl and index health, and branded versus non-branded demand where the data supports that split. Treat rank as a contextual diagnostic, not the primary outcome.
2. Build a query and topic map from first-party Search Console or webmaster exports, site search, support and sales language, customer research, and bounded manual observation of accessible public results. Separate observed demand from hypotheses and record locale, device, date, source, and sampling limits.
3. For each valuable topic, identify user intent, journey stage, current landing page, competing result types, missing or weak coverage, and the original evidence, experience, tool, comparison, or explanation the product can contribute. Do not copy competitor expression or infer demand from one personalized result page.
4. Prioritize work by audience value, business fit, evidence strength, achievable differentiation, authority and expertise, implementation effort, and risk. Prefer improving or consolidating useful pages over creating overlapping keyword variants.
5. Inventory indexable content and define what must remain private, excluded, canonical, redirected, consolidated, or retired. Authentication and authorization protect private data; crawler directives do not.
6. Design stable, human-understandable URLs and crawlable internal links. Return accurate HTTP status codes and preserve redirect mappings during migrations. Prevent faceted navigation, internal search, duplicates, staging, and deleted content from creating unbounded or misleading URL spaces.
7. Ensure each indexable route provides substantial semantic content and accurate metadata under the rendering conditions supported by target crawlers. Define useful titles and descriptions, document language, canonical and alternate relationships, and accessible media alternatives. Test rendered output rather than assuming JavaScript execution.
8. Add robots directives, sitemaps, and structured data from explicit policy. Structured data must truthfully represent visible content and a currently supported feature; valid syntax or eligibility does not guarantee display.
9. Publish through an accountable editorial process with named ownership, source and claim review, update triggers, and clear evidence of original value. AI assistance does not excuse unsupported claims, synthetic experience, mass low-value pages, or weak editorial oversight.
10. Measure crawl, index, search appearance, qualified traffic, conversion, and technical errors over an appropriate window. Annotate releases, compare similar periods or cohorts, check for confounders, and retain rollback for migrations or material template changes.

## Research Route

Prefer data from properties the user controls, official webmaster interfaces and APIs, customer-language evidence, source-owner feeds, and separately reviewed licensed datasets. Keep result-page observation small, documented, and non-automated unless an official or explicitly authorized API permits automation.

If a source presents an access restriction or anti-bot control, stop and reroute through the Research Standard. Do not rotate proxies or identities, forge user agents or sessions, defeat CAPTCHAs, evade rate limits, bypass paywalls or logins, or use cached or mirrored copies to defeat the source's restriction.

## Guardrails

- `robots.txt` is a crawler request protocol, not authorization or confidentiality.
- No standard, tool, consultant, paid submission, or markup guarantees crawling, indexing, rich results, or ranking.
- Do not create doorway pages, deceptive structured data, hidden content, link schemes, keyword stuffing, copied or lightly transformed pages, or mass low-value content.
- Do not automate queries against a search interface that prohibits machine-generated traffic. Review a rank-tracking or research provider as a third-party service before transmitting queries, URLs, account data, or payment information.
- Public visibility does not establish permission to scrape, retain, train on, or republish content. Minimize collection and preserve provenance, terms, licensing, and privacy boundaries.
- Connecting webmaster tools, analytics, or external verification accounts and transmitting production data is R3.
- Optimize for users, accessibility, performance, truthful content, and durable information architecture before search-engine tricks.

## Completion

Deliver the dated baseline; query, intent, and topic map; content and competitor evidence with access provenance; prioritized page backlog; public-content and URL inventory; crawl and index rules; rendering and metadata checks; canonical and redirect plan; sitemap and structured-data scope; multilingual coordination; validation evidence; measurement and privacy plan; migration rollback; owners; assumptions; and unresolved risks. Mark findings as observed, inferred, proposed, implemented, or verified.
