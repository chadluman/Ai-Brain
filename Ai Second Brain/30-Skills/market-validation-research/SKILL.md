# Market Validation Research

## Outcome

Produce a decision-ready evidence brief that separates observed user and market facts from assumptions, estimates, and advocacy, then recommends the smallest next test or a justified stop.

## Required Inputs

- decision to be made, decision owner, deadline, and evidence threshold
- problem hypothesis, affected groups, buyer, user, payer, and geographic scope
- current alternatives, workarounds, switching conditions, and known competitors
- intended offer, channel, price hypothesis, and stage of commitment
- available research, analytics, support evidence, public data, time, and budget
- privacy, accessibility, consent, age, regulated-domain, and contact constraints

## Safety and Permission Constraints

- Do not invent interviews, demand, willingness to pay, market size, conversion, competitor facts, or statistical confidence. Keep observations, reported claims, estimates, and inferences distinct.
- Public interest, search volume, sign-ups, survey intent, and total population are not equivalent to recurring demand or reachable revenue.
- Do not contact people, buy data or ads, scrape restricted sources, publish a test, create accounts, or collect personal data without authorization. Apply consent, minimization, access, retention, and deletion controls to research data.
- Include relevant disabled, low-connectivity, low-literacy, assisted, and non-digital users. Do not generalize from convenient participants to an excluded population.
- Do not present this work as legal, investment, financial, or regulated-market advice. Escalate jurisdiction-sensitive claims and consequential commitments to qualified reviewers.

## Workflow

1. State the decision, the claim that would justify it, the evidence needed, and the date after which the evidence becomes stale.
2. Define the segment operationally: situation, need, behavior, ability and authority to act, geography, and exclusions. Separate user, buyer, payer, approver, and beneficiary when they differ.
3. Inventory existing evidence before gathering more. Record source, date, method, sample, conflicts of interest, population, and limits.
4. Map the current journey and alternatives, including manual work, adjacent products, internal tools, postponement, and doing nothing. Compare outcomes and switching costs, not feature counts alone.
5. Frame falsifiable hypotheses for problem frequency and severity, present behavior, reachable demand, willingness to change or pay, acquisition path, retention, and delivery feasibility.
6. Estimate market bounds from explicit inputs. Prefer bottom-up reachable units and adoption scenarios; show formulas, ranges, uncertainty, and source dates rather than a single top-down headline.
7. Select the cheapest ethical test for the highest-risk hypothesis: evidence review, observation, interview, prototype, concierge test, price conversation, or authorized demand experiment. Define success, guardrail, and stop thresholds before seeing results.
8. Triangulate behavioral and attitudinal evidence. Look for disconfirming cases, sampling bias, nonresponse, novelty effects, interviewer influence, and alternative explanations.
9. Conclude with proceed, revise, pause, or stop. Name what is known, contradicted, unresolved, and the smallest next evidence step; do not convert weak evidence into a build mandate.

## Required Output

- decision statement, segment definition, actors, geography, and evidence date
- evidence register with method, sample, provenance, confidence, and limitations
- current journey, alternatives, competitor and substitute comparison, and switching conditions
- falsifiable hypotheses ranked by uncertainty and consequence
- market-sizing model with formula, ranges, sources, and reachable-scope assumptions
- experiment plan or results with consent, success, guardrail, and stop criteria
- proceed-revise-pause-stop recommendation and unresolved risks

## Completion Gate

Do not call the market validated until the target segment and decision are explicit, important claims have more than promotional or self-reported support where practical, contradictory evidence is visible, and the recommendation follows predeclared thresholds. A useful result may be to stop.
