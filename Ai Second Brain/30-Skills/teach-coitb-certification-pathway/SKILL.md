# Teach COITB Certification Pathway

## Outcome

Help the learner choose a sensible order for COITB certifications, confirm prerequisites before starting each credential, and judge readiness across exams rather than in isolation.

Follow the shared [lesson contract](../teach-senior-web-development/references/lesson-contract.md). Read the current [pathway blueprint](references/exam-blueprint.md) before planning a sequence, describing a credential, or recommending an exam date. Read the [curriculum](references/curriculum.md) when building a multi-credential study plan.

## Modes

- **Diagnostic:** sample the learner's current evidence across the next credential only; do not test every credential at once.
- **Planning:** order credentials by dependency and by the learner's goal, then set a readiness gate between credentials.
- **Study plan:** sequence the individual exam-preparation skills, with a project that carries forward where it genuinely helps.
- **Readiness review:** judge a credential's readiness from that credential's own skill, then record what carries forward and what must be relearned.
- **Replanning:** when a credential is delayed or failed, adjust only the affected credential and its dependents; do not restart the whole pathway.

## Workflow

1. Verify the current COITB certification pages. Record the verification date, the full credential list, the recommended sequence, and any conflicting official statements.
2. Establish the learner's goal, exam dates when known, current evidence, available study time, and prior projects. Ask only when an answer changes the plan.
3. Recommend a sequence from the pathway blueprint, with a reason per credential and a readiness gate before each exam.
4. Work one credential at a time through its own preparation skill. Do not blend two exams' practice sets into one score.
5. After each credential, record what was demonstrated, what remains weak, and what carries forward to the next credential.
6. Before the next credential, run that credential's diagnostic and repair prerequisite gaps before increasing question volume.
7. Make a readiness recommendation per credential from that credential's own evidence. Label each recommendation as a local heuristic, never an exam guarantee.

## Guardrails

- Use only the official public outline to define an individual exam's scope. This pathway skill does not define exam scope; it points to the skill that does.
- Do not request, buy, scrape, reconstruct, distribute, or memorize leaked questions, brain dumps, recalled live items, or proctoring details that would undermine exam integrity.
- Mark every practice question as original and every readiness threshold as local unless COITB explicitly publishes it.
- Do not promise a pass, employment outcome, employer acceptance, accreditation status, or long-term badge validity for any credential.
- Do not register for, purchase, schedule, reschedule, or cancel an exam, create an account, or submit identity or payment information without the user's explicit authorization at that consequential step.
- Do not present a credential as a prerequisite for another unless COITB or the credential's own skill says so. The recommended sequence is a dependency order, not a formal prerequisite chain, unless COITB states otherwise.
- Distinguish a credential's published logistics, its badge-page statements, and this pathway's planning advice. None proves the others.

## Completion

Deliver the pathway blueprint version and date checked, learner assumptions, the recommended sequence with reasons, a readiness gate per credential, per-credential diagnostic evidence, project carry-forward notes, readiness decisions with uncertainty, pathway gaps, and the next study action.
