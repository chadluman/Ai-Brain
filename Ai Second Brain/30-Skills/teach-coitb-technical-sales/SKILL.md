# Teach COITB Technical Sales

## Outcome

Help the learner become ready for the COITB Technical Sales Specialist certification and demonstrate the underlying skills in an original, end-to-end sales scenario: discovery, solution mapping, demo, objection handling, proposal, and follow-through.

Follow the shared [lesson contract](../teach-senior-web-development/references/lesson-contract.md). Read the current [exam blueprint](references/exam-blueprint.md) before planning, scoring, or describing the certification. Read the [curriculum](references/curriculum.md) when building a study plan, multi-session course, capstone, or mock-assessment sequence.

## Supporting Skills

Load only what the learner's gap requires:

- [Product Discovery and Scope](../product-discovery-scope/SKILL.md) for understanding a customer's problem before proposing a solution.
- [Market Validation Research](../market-validation-research/SKILL.md) for evidence about a market and a buyer, beyond anecdote.
- [Customer Support Readiness](../customer-support-readiness/SKILL.md) for handoff, follow-through, and the experience after a sale.
- [Teach Web Foundations](../teach-web-foundations/SKILL.md) for the technology vocabulary a technical seller needs to explain a product accurately.

## Modes

- **Diagnostic:** sample every curriculum area with original questions and one small scenario task; report evidence by area rather than one opaque score.
- **Teaching:** use concept → demo → guided scenario → independent variation → verification → original exam-style check.
- **Study plan:** prioritize gaps using measured weakness, since COITB does not publish domain weights or exam logistics for this credential on the sources checked for this skill; do not invent them to fill that gap.
- **Practice assessment:** create original multiple-choice and scenario-style questions mapped to the curriculum areas; reveal answers and rationales after the learner attempts them.
- **Scenario review:** map observed discovery notes, demos, and proposals to both the curriculum areas and the broader criteria explicitly included in the curriculum.

## Workflow

1. Verify the current COITB badge and exam-outline pages. Record the verification date, exam title, duration, item count, passing score, domains, weights, and any conflicting official statements. If the outline and logistics still cannot be located, say so plainly and treat the curriculum areas in this skill as preparation topics, not as the official exam scope.
2. Establish the learner's exam date when known, current evidence, available study time, prior sales or technical experience, and weakest perceived areas. Ask only when an answer changes the plan.
3. Run a short diagnostic across all curriculum areas. Do not infer readiness from confidence or a polished pitch alone.
4. Order instruction by dependency while allocating review and practice by measured weakness.
5. Grow one original sales scenario through the curriculum. Require the learner to justify every product claim with evidence before presenting it.
6. Use mixed retrieval and timed original practice after focused modules. Classify missed items as knowledge, scenario interpretation, product-accuracy, ethics, time management, or careless-reading errors.
7. Make a readiness recommendation from repeated practice evidence, area floors, scenario behavior, and the learner's ability to explain decisions. Label the recommendation as a local heuristic, never an exam guarantee.

## Guardrails

- Use only the official public outline to define exam scope once it is located. Until then, label curriculum topics as preparation topics, not official domains.
- Do not request, buy, scrape, reconstruct, distribute, or memorize leaked questions, brain dumps, recalled live items, or proctoring details that would undermine exam integrity.
- Mark every practice question as original and every readiness threshold as local unless COITB explicitly publishes it.
- Do not promise a pass, employment outcome, employer acceptance, accreditation status, or long-term badge validity.
- Do not register for, purchase, schedule, reschedule, or cancel an exam, create an account, or submit identity or payment information without the user's explicit authorization at that consequential step.
- Do not invent product claims, pricing, or competitor comparisons for a practice scenario. Mark every scenario fact as fictional or sourced, and correct any claim the learner cannot evidence.
- Do not teach deceptive, high-pressure, or misleading selling. A sale that depends on a claim the seller cannot support is a defect to fix, not a technique to copy.
- Distinguish a product's actual capability, a customer's stated need, and a seller's interpretation. None proves the others.

## Completion

Deliver the official blueprint version and date checked, learner assumptions, area-level diagnostic evidence, prioritized plan, scenario and practice artifacts, answer rationales after attempts, readiness decision with uncertainty, exam-scope gaps, and the next study action. For a final readiness review, report both weighted exam evidence (when official weights exist) and practical scenario evidence.
