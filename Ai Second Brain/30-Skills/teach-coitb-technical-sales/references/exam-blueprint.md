## Exam-to-Curriculum Interpretation

- Cover every curriculum area. Allocate practice by measured learner weakness, not by an invented weight.
- Teach scenario judgment as well as knowledge. A scenario-based exam can test discovery, solution fit, objection handling, and ethics without requiring a live customer.
- Pair each area with a working scenario artifact. Correct answers without the ability to run a discovery conversation, give an honest demo, and write a defensible proposal are insufficient practical evidence.
- Teach ethics as a cross-cutting expectation: accurate claims, disclosed limits, and respect for a customer's decision, even though no separate ethics domain is claimed here.
- Treat product-specific knowledge as scenario-specific. Teach the method for learning a product accurately, not a fictional product's feature list.

## Local Readiness Heuristic

This is a teaching heuristic, not a COITB policy or score prediction:

- complete at least three original mixed practice sets under time pressure;
- score at least 85% on each of the last two sets;
- score at least 75% within every curriculum area rather than relying on strength in one area to conceal a gap;
- explain the rationale for corrected answers without memorizing wording;
- complete the curriculum capstone acceptance criteria and diagnose at least one injected discovery, product-accuracy, demo, objection, and proposal defect.

If the exam date is near, prioritize high-weakness areas. If time permits, repair prerequisite gaps before increasing question volume.
