# COITB Technical Sales Curriculum

Use this as an adaptable exam-preparation sequence, not a fixed calendar. These are preparation topics, not claimed official COITB domains; order instruction by dependency and allocate practice by measured weakness. Build one original sales scenario throughout so knowledge checks remain connected to working behavior.

## Learner Assumptions

- The learner can explain a technical product in plain language at a basic level; teach the technology vocabulary first if the diagnostic shows a gap.
- All scenario companies, products, prices, and customer facts are fictional unless explicitly sourced. No real customer's information is used.
- Practice content is original, licensed, or explicitly permitted.
- Exam preparation and certification are separate outcomes. Completing this curriculum does not guarantee a passing score.

## Final Observable Outcome

The learner can answer original questions across all curriculum areas and complete an end-to-end sales scenario containing a discovery summary, a solution map tied to evidenced needs, an honest demo outline, an objection log with evidence-based responses, a written proposal with disclosed limits, and a handoff note for support.

## Scenario Spine

Use a single fictional scenario such as a small business choosing a web application, a team adopting a developer tool, or a nonprofit selecting a hosting and analytics setup:

- `discovery.md`: customer context, goals, constraints, and open questions, with sources marked fictional or sourced;
- `solution-map.md`: each need mapped to a product capability, a limit, or an explicit gap;
- `demo-outline.md`: the demo flow, what it will and will not claim, and the evidence behind each claim;
- `proposal.md`: scope, price basis, limits, and next steps, written so a non-technical buyer can follow it;
- `handoff.md`: what support receives, what was promised, and what was not.

No real product, price, or customer is required.

## Module 0 — Diagnostic and Exam Orientation

- **Outcome:** Produce area-level evidence and a prioritized study plan.
- **Demo:** Work through one original item by identifying the exact topic, eliminating distractors, and confirming the reasoning in the scenario.
- **Guided lab:** Inspect a deliberately uneven sample scenario for a discovery, product-accuracy, demo, objection, and proposal issue.
- **Independent task:** Complete a short original mixed diagnostic without notes.
- **Verification:** Record correct and incorrect results by area and error type; do not reduce the result to a single score.

## Module 1 — Discovery and Needs Analysis

- **Concepts:** open questions, listening and note-taking, goals and constraints, stakeholders, decision process, and what not to assume.
- **Demo:** Convert a vague customer request into a discovery summary with open questions.
- **Guided lab:** Write the scenario's discovery summary and mark every fact as evidenced or assumed.
- **Independent task:** Rewrite a pitch-first discovery into a needs-first one.
- **Original exam practice:** identify missing discovery, choose the next best question, and spot an assumed need.
- **Verification:** every proposed solution traces to an evidenced need; no assumed need drives a recommendation.

## Module 2 — Solution Mapping and Demo

- **Concepts:** capability mapping, limits and gaps, demo structure, evidence for claims, and when to recommend against a product.
- **Demo:** Map one need to a capability, a limit, and a gap, and say each plainly.
- **Guided lab:** Build the scenario's solution map and demo outline with evidence behind each claim.
- **Independent task:** Add a feature request the product cannot meet, and write the honest response.
- **Original exam practice:** identify overstated claims, choose the accurate demo claim, and spot a missing limit.
- **Verification:** every demo claim has evidence; every material limit is disclosed before the proposal.

## Module 3 — Objections, Proposal, and Follow-Through

- **Concepts:** objection types, evidence-based responses, proposal structure, price basis, negotiation ethics, handoff, and follow-up.
- **Demo:** Respond to a price objection with evidence and a scope choice, not pressure.
- **Guided lab:** Write the scenario's objection log, proposal, and handoff note.
- **Independent task:** Rewrite a pressure-based close as an evidence-based next step.
- **Original exam practice:** choose the ethical response to an objection, identify a misleading proposal claim, and spot a broken handoff.
- **Verification:** the proposal matches what was discovered and demoed; support receives everything it needs to keep the promises made.

## Module 4 — Review and Capstone

- **Concepts:** scenario review, claim auditing, and readiness evidence.
- **Demo:** Audit a completed scenario for unsupported claims before presenting it.
- **Guided lab:** Run a claim audit across the learner's full scenario and fix every unsupported claim.
- **Independent task:** Fix three injected defects, one each in discovery, demo, and proposal, and record what proved each fix.
- **Original exam practice:** mixed timed sets across all areas, with rationales after attempts.
- **Verification:** the capstone meets the final observable outcome and the learner can explain every major decision in it.
