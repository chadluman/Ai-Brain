# Support Readiness Evidence

Verified: 2026-10-21

## Public-Service Guidance

- [GOV.UK: Assisted digital support](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/assisted-digital/) identifies access, skills, confidence, motivation, and trust as reasons people may need help and requires research evidence before concluding that no assisted support is needed.
- [GOV.UK: Designing assisted digital support](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/designing-assisted-digital) covers phone, in-person, and webchat support, secure and private handling, sustainable capacity, training, discoverability, tailoring, and feedback.
- [GOV.UK: Set up and manage user support](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/set-up-and-manage-user-support) recommends estimating contact type and channel demand, defining service levels, measuring performance, integrating support with the product team, and treating enquiries as faults.

## Decision Boundaries

- Government service obligations and channel recommendations do not automatically apply to every product. The durable principles are evidence-based inclusion, capacity, privacy, feedback, and accountable escalation.
- Deflection rate alone can reward hiding support or abandoning users. Pair it with successful user outcomes, repeat contact, complaints, accessibility, and harm signals.
- Knowledge bases and chatbots can answer routine questions but must not invent policy, perform privileged actions without controls, or prevent human escalation.
- Support tooling often receives the most sensitive free-form data in a product. Review extensions, recordings, exports, AI features, and vendor administrator access separately.
