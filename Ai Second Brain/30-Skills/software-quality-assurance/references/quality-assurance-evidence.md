# Quality Assurance Evidence

Verified: 2026-10-22

## Definitions and Management Boundary

- [ISO 9000:2026](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:9000:ed-5:v1:en) is the current quality-management vocabulary. It distinguishes quality planning, assurance, control, and improvement: assurance provides confidence that quality requirements will be fulfilled, while control focuses on fulfilling those requirements.
- [ISO 9001:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/9001) is the current quality-management-system requirements standard. ISO's public material emphasizes leadership, quality culture, accountability, risks and opportunities, controlled operations, performance evaluation, and improvement. Certification is voluntary; this skill does not claim conformity or reproduce the paid standard.
- [ISO 19011:2026](https://www.iso.org/standard/19011) provides current guidance for management-system audit principles, audit programmes, audit conduct, evidence, risk, independence, competence, reporting, and improvement. It is audit guidance, not a certifiable standard and not proof that a software product is fit for release.

## Software Quality Planning

- [ISO/IEC 25010:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html) defines a product-quality model with nine characteristics for specifying and evaluating ICT and software quality. Use the model to test completeness, not as a universal checklist or an instruction to weight every characteristic equally.
- [ISO/IEC 25019:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78177.html) separates quality in use from inherent product properties and makes the specified context of use a prerequisite. Revisit quality-in-use requirements when users, tasks, devices, environments, or consequences change.
- [ISO/IEC 25030:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/72116.html) provides the current quality-requirements framework and covers elicitation, definition, governance, traceability, and testing considerations. ISO records that the edition was confirmed in 2025.
- [ISO/IEC 25020:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/72117.html) provides a quality-measurement framework, including measure selection, construction, reliability, validity, planning, execution, and use of results. A metric is useful only when its decision, conditions, uncertainty, and response are defined.

## Software Assurance Interpretation

- [ISO/IEC 20246:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/67407.html), confirmed in 2022, provides a generic framework for work-product reviews across the lifecycle. Review scope, technique, entry criteria, competence, independence, and evidence should be proportional to risk.
- [NIST SSDF 1.1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final) supplies secure-development practices for the security subset of software quality. It does not replace broader functional, accessibility, reliability, usability, maintainability, or quality-in-use requirements.

## Interpretation Boundaries

- Quality assurance reduces uncertainty; it cannot guarantee zero defects or eliminate the need for product evaluation.
- More documents, approvals, tests, or metrics do not automatically mean more assurance. Retain a control only when it addresses a named risk, requirement, decision, or learning need.
- Independence can improve defect detection but can also add delay or distance from context. Scale it to consequence and conflict of interest rather than requiring a separate team for all work.
- Process measures are vulnerable to target gaming. Pair them with user-visible outcomes, escaped defects, support evidence, incidents, and qualitative review.
- Standards define reusable models and processes. Project-specific thresholds, acceptance criteria, legal duties, and risk decisions still require current contextual evidence.
