# Content Moderation Evidence

Verified: 2026-10-22

## Regulatory and Distribution Sources

- [European Commission DSA questions and answers](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/digital-services-act-questions-and-answers) describes points of contact, qualified complaint handling, clear reasons, internal appeals, and out-of-court dispute options for covered services. Exact applicability and obligations require current legal review.
- [Ofcom online-safety regulatory documents](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-regulatory-documents) is the current index for United Kingdom risk-assessment guidance, codes, risk registers, and superseded versions. At verification, new illegal-content codes dated September 2026 replaced earlier versions, demonstrating the need for versioned policy evidence.
- [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) section 1.2 requires covered user-generated-content apps to include filtering, reporting and timely response, blocking, and contact mechanisms and was updated in 2026 for random or anonymous chat. It is a living distribution policy, not a universal moderation standard.
- [Google Play user-generated-content policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937) requires covered apps to define prohibited content, obtain acceptance of terms, conduct ongoing moderation appropriate to the content, and provide reporting and blocking capabilities for specified interactions. Full current policy controls over summaries.

## Decision Implications

- Store rules, service terms, and law overlap but are not interchangeable. Build an applicability matrix by feature, audience, jurisdiction, and distribution channel.
- Content risk is shaped by product architecture: reach, recommendation, virality, anonymity, direct contact, monetization, live interaction, and age access can change severity and required controls.
- Removal is only one control. Safer defaults, reach limits, blocking, friction, age controls, reporting, reasons, appeal, restoration, and incident response are independent parts of the system.
- Transparency and due process require structured reason codes and evidence, but disclosure can sometimes enable evasion or endanger people. Define authorized exceptions and review instead of omitting the design.
- Regulations and store policies changed during 2026 and will continue changing. Recheck the exact source and effective date before launch, major feature change, or store submission.
